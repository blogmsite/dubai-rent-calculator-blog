The five permitted bands

Under Decree 43/2013, the permitted increase depends on how far your current rent is below the average for similar homes in the Smart Rental Index. The bands themselves are 0 / 5 / 10 / 15 / 20% — there is no permitted increase above 20%. Which band applies to you is set by the Index gap for your unit, so check your exact figure in the official DLD calculator before agreeing or refusing. Ejari renewal, step by step

  1. Check the increase first. Run the checker above and confirm your permitted band in the official DLD Smart Rental Index before you agree to any new rent.
  2. Count the notice. An increase needs 90 days' notice. If you were told later than that, use the short-notice letter above.
  3. Agree the new rent in writing. Keep the agreed figure, dates and cheque schedule in the signed tenancy contract — not just in chat messages.
  4. Renew the Ejari. Register the renewed contract so the Ejari certificate matches the new term and rent.
  5. Keep everything. Contract, Ejari certificate, cheques and the landlord's notice together — they are what a Rental Dispute Centre case would turn on.

Guides

How the RERA increase actually works

Your landlord cannot simply pick a number. The increase is capped by Decree 43/2013 into five bands — 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% — based on how far your rent sits below the Smart Rental Index average for similar property. The further below the average you are, the higher the permitted band; if you are already at or near the average, the permitted increase can be 0%. Two protections matter most in practice: the 20% ceiling, and the 90-day notice rule. If either is breached, you have grounds to push back before signing — use the letters above.

Sources: Driven Properties; PropertyStellar (accessed 6 Nov 2026).

Rent vs buy in Dubai: what the JVC maths really says

MortgageCompare's worked example (updated 2 Nov 2026) puts a JVC 1-bed at AED 55,000 a year in rent against a mortgage of AED 3,333 a month — AED 39,996 a year — with a break-even of 3–6 years and about 28% of the price needed in cash upfront. Read that carefully: buying is cheaper per month in the example, but only wins if you stay past the break-even, because the upfront cash is real money out on day one. Run your own area through the calculator above with your real mortgage quote — do not copy JVC numbers onto a Marina or Downtown decision.
